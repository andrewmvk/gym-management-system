# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Next.js (TypeScript) frontend with Tailwind CSS + shadcn/ui; Node.js + Express backend exposing tRPC (typed API shared with the frontend); Drizzle ORM over PostgreSQL; custom JWT auth via httpOnly cookie; OpenRouter API (configurable free-tier model) for all AI calls; a real transactional email provider (e.g. Resend) for the registration-completed email with the login link; face-detection/embedding library (e.g. face-api.js), run on the backend for the signup reference embedding and in the browser for kiosk check-in; local disk file storage via a Docker volume. Two Docker Compose containers, as the course requires: frontend, and backend (Node API and PostgreSQL together). The kiosk check-in panel is the same Next.js app running in a dedicated route, not a separate container. This stack was pre-decided in `docs/04-architecture.md` before this record was written, not chosen during this interview.

## Users

Single gym, three roles:

- **Member**: is accepted at the physical gym by staff (off-system), registers there, completes the required onboarding, receives a personalized AI-generated daily plan, checks in via face recognition, works with the AI coach as a custom trainer (asks about, points at and changes their plan, understands any exercise, and always applies the changes themselves), edits their own sets, reps and weight, sees and confirms or corrects what the coach remembers, reads their trainers' notes, views history and metrics.
- **Personal Trainer** (gym staff): oversees AI-generated plans across all members as a shared pool (no 1:1 assignment); sees the member context the AI worked from (health profile, remembered facts, exams) to judge safety; can comment on or directly override any plan that is not in the past, and the member reads those notes.
- **Gym Admin** (gym staff/management): configures the external turnstile REST API integration and watches its health through the check-in log, manages the exercise/equipment catalog, edits the opening hours, monitors gym occupancy and demand, and switches each member's mocked membership status between active and inactive.

## Product Purpose

Replaces two disconnected manual processes at a physical gym: a fingerprint scanner with no identity intelligence, and a human trainer who verbally interviews each member on the spot and can't scale or remember history between sessions. The product unifies both into an AI-driven system: face recognition for frictionless access, and a continuously-learning AI coach, the member's custom trainer, that builds a training plan from a rich onboarding profile (physical information, goals, medication, conditions, exams) and ongoing conversation, refining it over time and spreading members across the gym's equipment using the demand of other members' plans. Success means personalization that's available 24/7 and informed by accumulated state (injuries, medication, life events), not just a single check-in. Deciding whether a person may train at all is not the product's job: staff do that in person at the gym before the person registers.

## Positioning

The mechanism a competing product could not casually copy: the AI's plan is built from an accumulating structured fact history (every chat message is mined for durable facts - skipped exercises, new injuries, medication changes, life events - and persisted permanently), not from a single interview or a session-scoped conversation. This is combined with frictionless face-recognition access that integrates with the gym's existing turnstile hardware/API, while keeping a human trainer in the loop asynchronously so the AI never has to wait on staff to publish a plan. The facts the AI remembers are visible to the member, who can mark one no longer true, and the trainers' notes are visible to the member.

## Operating Context

Single gym, single physical location - no multi-tenancy in the data or auth model. A kiosk-style web app runs at the gym's physical entry panel (the same Next.js app, dedicated route) and is treated as a less-trusted client than the authenticated app. The whole system targets local Docker Compose only; there is no production deployment target. **This is an academic project and will not be deployed to real users**: member data, biometric data (face embeddings/photos), and the turnstile REST API are mocked or seeded for evaluation; no real payments are processed and no real biometric data is collected from real people. All "today"/date logic (plan dates, occupancy window, gym-open hours) uses the server's local system timezone - no per-user timezone handling. Registration is meant to happen on the gym's own devices, after staff accept the person in person; for this academic project that restriction exists only on paper and is not enforced.

## Capabilities and Constraints

Full functional detail lives in `docs/02-requirements.md` (FR-1 through FR-72) and `docs/03-features-and-flows.md`; durable constraints worth restating here:

- In-person registration: whether a person may train is decided by staff at the physical gym (they check workout restrictions and any medical certificate in person), and only then is the person sent to registration. That check is off-system and leaves no record: there is no digital questionnaire, AI verdict, certificate upload, review queue or rejected applicant. Registration collects details, explicit biometric consent, a webcam reference photo (embedding computed server-side, raw photo never leaves the backend) and a password, written in one request; completing it creates the account at once with an active mocked membership and sends an email with a link to the login page. The registration page is meant to be reachable only at the gym, but that restriction exists only on paper and is not enforced.
- Onboarding is required: after login a member who has not completed it is redirected there from every member route. It collects physical information (height and weight), goals, medications, conditions, medical exams (structured entries with the findings typed by the member, plus an optional attached file the AI never reads, since the AI carries text only) and other notes, feeds the first AI training plan, and is not one-time - it can be extended later by form or chat. The newest submission is the current truth.
- Plan generation reads the member's current profile, remembered facts (including the plan requests the member made), the last 14 days of plans with completion, the member's trainer notes, equipment and exercise availability and the demand of other members' plans for that date; safety, injuries, medication and exams always override preferences and demand. Demand is for the AI and for staff, never public.
- Remembered facts are visible to the member, who can mark one no longer true; the AI skips resolved facts but always gets injuries and medication changes in detail. An injury or a medication change the coach extracts from chat is only a candidate until the member confirms it (in the chat, or later on the Health profile), and an injury carries the muscles it affects so it shows on the muscle map. Every other fact, such as a wish for more of a muscle, is remembered at once without asking, and a plan is remembered as one short line only when the member applies it. The member's own edits of sets, reps and weight are remembered too, so the coach learns what they could or could not do.
- The coach is the center of the product, and the member stays in control of it: it answers in text and in typed components (a plan proposal the member can read as a diff and edit, an exercise picker, an explainer, a safety warning, an injury or medication to confirm), the member can point at an exact exercise, muscle, muscle group or the whole muscle distribution through one reference system, and nothing the coach proposes changes a plan until the member applies it. The coach has read-only lookups and no write path to the database. A safety warning about a reported injury must be accepted to apply an exercise, and the trainers see what members changed through the coach, flagged when a warning was accepted. Replies and plan builds stream, and a stream that breaks saves nothing.
- Plans publish immediately, never blocked on trainer review (preserves 24/7 value prop); trainer review is asynchronous, shared-pool (not 1:1), and multi-note (later notes never overwrite earlier ones). Trainer notes are visible to the member and used by the AI as guidance. Regenerating a trainer-edited plan, or one with exercises already ticked off, requires member confirmation first; ticked exercises are never silently lost, and a past plan is history that trainers cannot edit. An AI failure never publishes a plan.
- Exercises come from a curated, add-only library (never deleted, so historical plans always resolve); an exercise is includable only if it needs no equipment or at least one linked equipment item is currently available. Every exercise carries a muscle map (primary and supporting muscles from one fixed list of 22), which drives the muscle heat maps for plans, metrics, gym demand and catalog coverage; the six muscle groups (chest, shoulders, back, arms, core, legs) group them for the member's radar and for the coach. What a member wants emphasized is a remembered plan request, a preference that safety and health history always override.
- Chat is stateless/session-less in the UI (FR-25/26) - no persisted visible chat log - but every message is mined for structured facts that, once the member confirms them, persist permanently in the member's history; this is what "remembers" the member across future plan generations.
- Kiosk performs 1:N face-embedding matching only; it fetches `{memberId, embedding}` pairs from a dedicated, kiosk-authenticated endpoint and never receives raw photos. Ambiguous matches (top two too close to call) are rejected, not guessed. The kiosk is the least trusted client and never shows another person's name; after three consecutive misses it sends the person to the front desk.
- Turnstile hardware call is best-effort: check-in is always recorded with a success/failed tag regardless of whether the external API call succeeds, and the kiosk tells the person that staff will open the door. Admins see the result of every check-in in a check-in log.
- Membership is a mocked active/inactive status: an inactive member cannot log in and cannot check in at the kiosk (no turnstile call, no check-in recorded) until an admin reactivates them; no payment is processed.
- No checkout event anywhere - occupancy is a rolling-window estimate, explicitly framed as such, not an exact headcount.
- Trainer/admin accounts exist only via a first-boot seed script with fixed demo credentials - there is no staff self-signup UI anywhere. Permissions themselves are policy-driven (member/trainer/admin are seeded policy groups, a named bundle of policies a user belongs to, not a fixed role field), so an Admin can later change what an existing account can do without that being a self-signup path.
- Explicitly out of scope: real payments/billing, building the turnstile API itself, a persisted/scrollable chat log, multi-location support, 1:1 trainer assignment, real biometric data from real people, a real checkout/exit flow, enforcing the registration restriction (intranet, IP allowlist or staff-issued token), any digital aptitude screening or certificate handling, notifications to staff, exercise deletion or editing an exercise's muscle map after creation, password reset/account recovery, WCAG/i18n/offline/scale requirements, production infra hardening.

## Evidence on Hand

None real, by design. All member records, check-ins, training plans, and the turnstile API are mocked or seeded for an academic demo - future work must not fabricate real testimonials, case studies, benchmarks, pricing, or biometric data from real people. A seed script is the source of demo data (fixed trainer/admin accounts and a demo student member, initial exercise/equipment catalog, fake members/check-ins/plans for realistic occupancy/demand numbers, lapsed inactive members, remembered facts and trainer notes; every demo member has completed registration).

## Product Principles

1. **AI acts, humans oversee asynchronously.** Plans generate and publish immediately; trainer review never blocks publication. This asynchrony is what makes 24/7 personalization real, not aspirational. The coach never writes on its own: a change it proposes takes effect when the member applies it, and a plan still publishes without trainer approval.
2. **History over interviews.** Every future plan reasons from accumulated structured facts (injuries, medication, life events, adjustments), not a single onboarding conversation or the raw chat transcript.
3. **Minimize biometric exposure at each trust boundary.** Only the backend ever sees raw reference photos; only embeddings reach the kiosk, the least-trusted client.
4. **Distinguish technical failure from a real determination.** An AI outage or turnstile call failure is always its own state: a failed turnstile call is recorded as `failed`, and when the AI cannot answer, plan generation fails with an error - an AI failure never publishes a placeholder plan and is never silently coalesced into a real plan or access decision.
5. **Scope matches an academic, single-location demo.** No multi-tenancy, no production hardening, no payment processing - simplicity here is deliberate, not a gap to fill later.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No accessibility (WCAG), offline, internationalization, or scale requirement is established for this project (NFR-4) - explicitly out of scope for this academic, non-deployed demo.
