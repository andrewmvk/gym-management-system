# Requirements

See [01-product-overview.md](./01-product-overview.md) for roles and context.

## 1. Functional Requirements

### 1.1 Registration (in person)
- FR-1: A prospective member registers at the gym (FR-57) by submitting basic info: name, phone number, email, birthdate. Nothing is stored until the final registration submission (FR-9): the details, consent, reference photo and password are held in the browser and written together in one request, so a person who abandons registration midway leaves no record behind. The first step checks the e-mail without storing anything: an already registered e-mail is refused. Until that submission the person can go back to any earlier step and change what they entered. The registration steps are Details, Consent, Photo and Password.
- FR-2: As part of signup, the member captures a reference photo (webcam). The backend computes a face embedding from it; the embedding is what's used for later recognition, and the raw photo is retained server-side only (audit/recompute), never distributed to any client.
- FR-3: Removed 2026-10-04 (audit-log item 20): there is no digital aptitude/health questionnaire; whether a person may train is decided in person at the gym before registration (FR-57).
- FR-4: Removed 2026-10-04 (audit-log item 20): there is no AI aptitude verdict (`cleared` / `not_cleared` / `pending_retry`); the system no longer screens people digitally.
- FR-5: Removed 2026-10-04 (audit-log item 20): there is no medical certificate upload or AI certificate review; a certificate is shown to staff in person and leaves no record in the system.
- FR-6: Removed 2026-10-04 (audit-log item 20): there is no certificate backstop review queue, because no certificate results exist in the system.
- FR-7: Removed 2026-10-04 (audit-log item 20): there is no Admin confirmation or override of a certificate result; the staff decision happens at the gym.
- FR-8: Removed 2026-10-04 (audit-log item 20): there is no rejected applicant and no permanently blocked e-mail; a person staff do not accept at the gym is never sent to registration and nothing about them is stored.
- FR-9: Completing the registration (the final submission of FR-1) creates the account at once: the password the person chose (at least 8 characters) is stored as a hash, the member policy group is assigned and the mocked membership status is `active` (FR-40). An already registered e-mail is refused; there is no "rejected" state. Authentication exists only from this point, and the member then receives the registration-completed e-mail with a link to the login page (FR-10).
- FR-45: During signup, the member may optionally report their gender, used only to inform AI-generated training plan recommendations. It is never required to complete signup.
- FR-46: Before the backend computes a face embedding from the signup reference photo (FR-2), the member must give explicit, specific consent for biometric data processing (LGPD Art. 11 - a generic terms-of-use acceptance is not sufficient). Consent is recorded permanently - who, when, and which version of the consent text was shown - and an embedding is never computed without a recorded consent for that member. The consent is given on its own screen and sent with the final registration submission; the backend records it before computing the embedding, and a rejected photo rolls the whole submission back, consent included.
- FR-57: Registration is done in person at the gym. Accepting a person as a member is not the system's job: before anyone registers, a staff member asks about the person's workout restrictions, looks at any medical certificate they have, and decides in person; only then is the person sent to the registration page (`/signup`). That check leaves no record in the system: there is no digital questionnaire, verdict, certificate or applicant state. The registration page is meant to be reachable only from the gym's own devices or network, but for this academic project that restriction exists only on paper and is not enforced: there is no intranet, IP allowlist or staff-issued token (see Out of Scope).

### 1.2 Onboarding (post-authentication)
- FR-10: After registration (FR-9), the system sends the member an email containing a link to the login page, not to the onboarding form. After logging in, the member is taken to onboarding (FR-11).
- FR-11: Onboarding is required before using the app. It is a normal page inside the authenticated app, not only reachable after the emailed link: a member who has not completed onboarding is redirected there from every member route on login, so a lost, typo'd, or spam-filtered email is never a dead end. A signed-in member with a completed onboarding goes to Now (the home screen, FR-55).
- FR-12: The onboarding form lets the member submit: physical information (height in cm and weight in kg, both required), goals/pretensions, medications, physical conditions/limitations, medical exams, and any other free-form relevant info. A medical exam is a structured entry with a name, an optional date and a findings summary the member types, plus an optional attached file. The AI module carries text only, so the AI reads the typed findings; an attached file is optional supporting evidence for staff and is never read by the AI. A member with no exams or no medications says so ("none" is a valid answer).
- FR-13: Onboarding data is stored and used as the primary input for the member's first AI-generated training plan. A member's newest onboarding submission is always the current truth: later plans and the chat read it as the member's current profile.
- FR-14: Onboarding is not strictly one-time - a member can add/update this information later (via the AI chat, see 1.4).

### 1.3 Training Plans & Exercise/Equipment Catalog
- FR-15: The AI generates a training plan for the member from the member's context: the newest onboarding submission (goals, physical information, physical conditions, medications, exams), the remembered facts that still apply (FR-58), including the plan requests the member made, such as a wish for more of a muscle (FR-27), the last 14 days of plans with their exercises, sets x reps, load, done or not done and check-in dates, the member's latest trainer notes and edits (FR-19), and the demand of other members' plans for that date (FR-29), together with the availability of equipment and exercises (FR-17). Safety, injuries, medication and exams always override preferences, plan requests and demand. When the live AI returns nothing usable, generation fails with an error and no plan is saved (FR-59).
- FR-16: Training plans are composed of exercises selected from a curated, structured exercise library (not free-text), enabling per-exercise, per-muscle and per-equipment metrics. Every exercise carries a muscle map: one or more primary muscles and any number of supporting muscles, chosen from a fixed list of 22 muscles (FR-49), never a free-text muscle group.
- FR-17: Each exercise is linked to zero or more pieces of gym equipment. An exercise is only includable in a plan if it requires no equipment (bodyweight) or at least one of its linked equipment items is currently marked available. This must be checked both when generating a new plan and when the AI/member views whether an existing plan's exercises are still performable.
- FR-18: A generated plan is published and visible to the member immediately (no blocking trainer approval step).
- FR-19: Any trainer (shared pool, not a 1:1 assignment) can view any member's plan and leave a comment/note or edit/override it, at any time the plan is not in the past (FR-59). Each note and each edit is its own entry (author, time, text): multiple trainers may each leave their own notes over time, a later note never overwrites an earlier one, and all are retained as a history. The entries are readable by any staff member and by the member whose plan it is, in full, oldest first, with an edit flagged "Edited the plan"; the member's badge reads "Edited by a trainer" or, for a note without an edit, "Trainer note". The AI uses a member's trainer notes as guidance when it builds or rebuilds that member's plans (FR-15).
- FR-20: The member can view their plan for the current day, browse their full historical plan record by date, and see their upcoming plans, all on one plan page with no tabs. A day strip above the plan shows one week at a time (Monday to Sunday) with a quiet tile per day: the weekday and date, and under them what the day holds (a slanted segment per exercise, filled when done, a check when the whole plan is done, the done count in between, dashed segments for a day that has not come, and a small Pace Tape mark when a trainer edited it). The member moves a week at a time, jumps back to today, or jumps to any date; a day with no plan can be opened too. The plan below shows the chosen day: today's is ticked off, a past day is read-only, and a day ahead shows its ticks locked (a plan can only be ticked on the day). The plan header shows the day's completion (done of total, with progress segments), and every view shows the plan's muscle map (FR-50). An exercise's own note is behind a note icon on its row: a mouse peeks at it by hovering, and a click, a tap or the keyboard opens it and keeps it open.
- FR-21: The member (via chat) can ask for a plan adjustment for a given date - past (retroactive correction, e.g. "I didn't do X") or future (e.g. changing today's or an upcoming day's plan). The coach answers with a full proposal for that date (FR-65), in the same reply: there is no second AI call. Applying it is a member-triggered server mutation that re-validates the exercises (catalog, availability) and the member's permission before writing, so what the member previewed is exactly what is saved.
- FR-22: If applying a proposal, or rebuilding a plan with the AI, would replace a plan that already carries a trainer's direct edit, or that holds exercises the member already ticked off, the member must first be shown a warning that says which of the two it is and what it will replace, and must confirm before it proceeds. Automatic regeneration after onboarding never overwrites such a plan (FR-59).
- FR-23: A retroactive correction directly overwrites that date's historical plan/exercise record - this is intentional (that is what "correction" means here), not a versioned/append-only history. The correction is a proposal like any other (FR-65) in which the coach states the completed tick of every exercise, and it is applied in place on the same plan row. Metrics (FR-36) always reflect the corrected version.
- FR-24: The Admin manages the exercise/equipment catalog: can add new exercises and add new gym equipment, and can set whether a piece of equipment is currently available, to an explicit value (not a flip). Before a piece is switched off, a confirmation shows its impact: how many plans dated today or later would become must-review (FR-53) and how many members they belong to. The Admin can also edit which exercises an equipment item is linked to; removing an exercise's last link makes it count as needing no equipment, so the screen warns before it. The catalog lists and the equipment endpoints are readable by any signed-in user, and the tab copy says what each view is based on. "Out of service" is the one user-facing phrase for unavailable equipment. A new exercise is tagged with its muscles at creation through a body selector (FR-49), never by typing a name. Admin cannot delete an exercise - exercises are add-only, so historical plans always resolve against a valid exercise - and its muscle map is not edited after creation.

### 1.4 AI Chat Assistant
- FR-25: The member can converse with an AI assistant at any time; the assistant has access to the member's full profile, onboarding data, remembered facts (FR-58) and history as context, assembled fresh on every request (no server-side conversation/session object). Beyond that base context it may call a small set of read-only lookups, each bound to the signed-in member: the details of one exercise, a search of the catalog by muscle, the member's recent workout history, the member's trainer notes and the equipment that is out of service. The AI has no write tools and never writes to the database: everything it produces is a proposal for the member to apply or a fact the server stores for it (FR-27, FR-65).
- FR-26: The chat UI itself does not need to persist/replay a visible conversation thread across sessions (no session management required). Within one open chat the browser sends the last few turns (the member's words and a one-line summary of each component the coach showed) with every message, so "yes, add it" and a retry after a failed reply keep their meaning; the server stores none of it, and plan generation still reasons from structured facts, never from this transcript.
- FR-27: Every chat message is processed to extract structured, durable facts (e.g., skipped exercise, new injury, medication change, reported life event like playing soccer, updated physical state). The facts that steer safety, an injury or a medication change, are stored as pending and only count, in plans and in chat, once the member confirms them, either in the chat (where they can be edited first) or on the Health profile (FR-72); one the member dismisses is discarded. Every other fact (a skipped exercise, a life event, an updated physical state, a wish such as "more back next time") is remembered at once without asking, and the member can mark it no longer true (FR-58). A request for a plan is not remembered when it is made: it is remembered when the member applies the plan (FR-66). An injury also carries the muscles it affects, so it can be drawn on the muscle map (FR-69) and checked against exercises (FR-28). The member can see and correct all confirmed facts (FR-58).
- FR-28: The AI can give feedback informed by this history (e.g., flag risk from an old injury given current exercises, adapt a plan because the member reports having played soccer). Injuries and medication changes are always given to the AI in detail, however old. Independently of the AI, an exercise whose primary muscle is one an injury names is flagged as a conflict deterministically, by the server, whenever a proposal contains it (FR-67).
- FR-29: When the AI builds or rebuilds a plan, it is given the demand of other members' plans for that plan date: how many plans need each piece of equipment (counted in plans, never exercise rows or sets) and the muscle load of those plans. It uses this to spread broad goals across different equipment and to avoid overloaded equipment. Safety, injuries, medication, exams and the member's plan requests always win over demand. If the member asks, the AI can also talk about aggregate info on other members' plans for the day (e.g., "what is everyone else doing today").

### 1.5 Facial Recognition Check-in
- FR-30: A kiosk-style web app (running at the gym's physical panel) captures a photo via webcam and computes its face embedding client-side.
- FR-31: The kiosk performs 1:N matching against embeddings only. It fetches a dataset of `{memberId, embedding}` pairs, one for each registered member with a stored embedding, from a dedicated, kiosk-authenticated backend endpoint - raw reference photos are never sent to or stored on the kiosk.
- FR-32: Matching applies a similarity/distance threshold to decide whether there's a match at all. If the best and second-best candidates are both within the threshold and too close to call (ambiguous), the match is rejected and the member is asked to retry, rather than guessing between them.
- FR-33: On an accepted match of a member whose membership is active (FR-40), the backend attempts to call the gym's existing external turnstile REST API to unlock the turnstile. Whether that call succeeds or fails, the check-in itself is always recorded, tagged with the outcome (`success`/`failed`). A member whose membership is `inactive` is refused: the API answers 403 with `reason: 'membership_inactive'`, no turnstile call is made and no check-in is recorded.
- FR-34: If the turnstile API call fails/times out, the kiosk says "Please wait. A staff member will open the door for you." with a small instruction for staff, so staff can open the turnstile manually - the visit is still counted as a check-in regardless. The kiosk is the least trusted, public-facing client, so it never shows another person's name; after 3 consecutive no-match or ambiguous results it says "Please see the front desk"; an inactive membership has its own status ("Membership inactive. Please see the front desk."); "Access not available" (solid red, a settled decision) is told apart from "Check-in unavailable" (the request failed, dashed); and no developer text (file paths, key names) is ever shown on the wall.
- FR-35: An Admin has a settings area to input/configure the values needed to call the external turnstile REST API (HTTP method, URL, headers such as credentials or an API key, and an optional request body) - the API itself is not built by this project.

### 1.6 Metrics & Gym Info
- FR-36: The member can view personal metrics: training frequency, days trained, exercises performed (breakdown), training volume, a muscle heat map of the completed work in the chosen range (weighted sets per muscle, FR-50), and progress toward stated goals/pretensions. "Days trained"/training frequency counts distinct calendar days with at least one recorded check-in (`f_check_ins`) - a day where only plan exercises were marked completed without a physical check-in does not count.
- FR-37: There is no checkout event anywhere in the system. "Current occupancy" is an estimate, computed as the count of distinct members with a check-in within a trailing rolling time window (e.g. the last 90 minutes) as of now - it is explicitly framed as an estimate, not an exact live headcount.
- FR-38: The public gym info page (`/gym`, readable without signing in) shows only: whether the gym is currently open, today's hours and when it next opens or closes, the occupancy estimate (FR-37) and, for anyone with the read-check-ins permission, today's check-ins per hour (distinct members per hour). It shows no muscle or equipment demand: demand is for the AI (FR-29) and for staff (FR-56). The page is linked from the public header, the landing page and the member and staff areas; staff also get links to the Overview and, for an admin, the check-in log (FR-61).
- FR-39: All "today"/date logic (plan dates, the occupancy window, gym open/closed hours) uses the server's local system timezone consistently - there is no per-user timezone handling.

### 1.7 Membership, Staff Accounts & Access Policies
- FR-40: Each member has a mocked membership status, `active` or `inactive`; registration sets it to `active` (FR-9). No real payment processing is implemented. `inactive` means the member cannot log in (the login fails with "Your membership is inactive. Ask the front desk to reactivate it." only after a correct password, and an existing session stops working) and cannot check in at the kiosk (FR-33). An Admin switches the status with a per-row control in the Members table and on the staff Member page (FR-60); deactivating asks for confirmation, and trainers see the status read-only. The scenario this serves: a member stops paying, comes back months later, pays at the front desk, and a staff member reactivates them.
- FR-41: Trainer and admin accounts are never created through a self-service signup flow. A seed script creates a fixed set of demo trainer/admin accounts with known credentials at first boot; there is no staff registration UI anywhere in the app. This is about account *creation* only - it does not restrict how an existing account's permissions can change afterward (see FR-43).
- FR-42: Every user's permissions are derived from the granular access policies currently in effect for them - those granted to them directly (`d_user_policy` rows referenced via `f_user_policy_on_user`) plus those of every policy group they currently belong to (FR-47) - see [05-data-model.md](./05-data-model.md). There is no fixed role field. A user with no active grant for a given permission cannot perform the action it covers, regardless of who they are.
- FR-43: A user holding the appropriate policy-management permission (e.g. an Admin) can view every defined policy and every user's current grants, and can grant, revoke or extend any policy - including the trainer or admin policy set - on any existing user. This only changes what an existing account can do; it never creates a new account, so FR-41's "no staff self-signup" boundary still holds. An admin can never remove their own access to the staff area or to policy management.
- FR-44: The onboarding gate (FR-11) and the member-only application area apply only to a user whose effective permissions include the member-designated policies (in practice, the Member group). There is no other gate: a person is either registered (FR-9) or has no account. A seeded trainer/admin account never receives these policies, so it's exempt from the gate and lands directly in the staff area instead.
- FR-47: Policies are bundled into named, seeded **policy groups** (`member`, `trainer`, `admin`), each a fixed list of policies (`d_user_policy_group` and `d_user_policy_group_policy`, see [05-data-model.md](./05-data-model.md)). A user belongs to any number of groups through `f_user_policy_group_on_user`, with an optional expiry. Belonging to a group has exactly the effect of holding every policy in it, for as long as the membership is active; the group is the source of truth, so changing a group's policy list changes every current member. A direct `denied` policy grant still overrides a policy that arrives through a group. Groups are read-only in the application: there is no screen or procedure to create, edit or delete one, and the seed owns their contents.
- FR-48: A user holding the policy-management permission can see, at a glance, which groups each user belongs to and where each of their effective policies comes from (a group or a direct grant); can assign a group to, revoke it from, or extend it for any existing user; and can filter the user list by group and by policy. Assigning a group never creates an account (FR-41).

### 1.8 Muscle Map
- FR-49: Muscles are one fixed list of 22, shared by the API and the web app (`packages/shared`): neck, chest, front delts, side delts, rear delts, biceps, triceps, forearm flexors, forearm extensors, abs, lower abs, obliques, traps, rotator cuff, lats, lower back, glutes, abductors, quads, outer quads, hamstrings and calves. Each muscle has a label and the body view or views it is drawn on (front, back, or both). Left and right are never told apart: a muscle is painted, hovered and selected as one, and a muscle visible from both sides carries the same weight on both views. An exercise is tagged with at least one primary muscle and any supporting muscles; a muscle is listed at most once per exercise. A primary muscle counts for 1 and a supporting muscle for 0.5 when work is weighed.
- FR-50: The member's plan page shows a muscle map of the plan: a front and back body in an achromatic five-step heat ramp, where each muscle's heat is its weighted sets (sets times the muscle's weight, FR-49) scaled against the busiest muscle, counting only exercises that are performable right now (FR-17). Beside the body is a radar chart of the six muscle groups (chest, shoulders, back, arms, core, legs), each point the weighted sets of its muscles; hovering or selecting a muscle or a point lights up the other, and each says its name and value in a tooltip. A ranked list of the muscles remains in the page, visually hidden, as the text summary and the keyboard path (and is shown where the exact per-muscle number is the point, as in the staff demand view). The map is a block inside the plan, between its progress and its exercises, with the body and the radar side by side from a medium width. Selecting a muscle on the body shows the exercises that train it and dims the other exercises. The same map appears for any day in plan history, and for the completed work of the chosen range in metrics (FR-36).
- FR-51: Withdrawn. The first version let the member set a per-muscle focus from -2 to +2. It was removed: what the member wants emphasized ("I feel like we could do more back next time") is a remembered plan request (FR-27), which plan generation and the coach already read (FR-15, FR-25), so a second setting only duplicated it. There is no focus control, no focus marker on the muscle map and no "rebuild with my focus" action; a plan changes only through a proposal the member applies (FR-65). The number is kept so the other references stay valid.
- FR-52: The staff catalog has a Coverage tab that shows how well the catalog covers each muscle, in three views: the whole catalog, only what is available now (FR-17), and what is lost to out-of-service equipment. A muscle no exercise trains is marked as a gap. Selecting a muscle lists its exercises with their availability and the equipment they use. A list of out-of-service equipment shows what each piece takes out; selecting one redraws the map to show only the muscles it hurts and the exercises it removes. The Equipment tab also states, for each out-of-service piece, which muscles it takes out.

### 1.9 Must-Review Plans, Plan Review Preview, Member Now and Staff Overview
- FR-53: A plan dated today or later that holds an exercise that cannot be done right now (FR-17: none of its linked equipment is available) is a **must-review plan** (a plan where the member accepted a safety warning is one too, until a trainer has looked at it, FR-71). The flag is derived on every read, never stored, so it clears by itself when the plan is edited or rebuilt or when the equipment comes back; a plan dated before today is never flagged. The member sees a notice on their plan and on the Now screen (FR-55) naming the exercises that cannot be done and the equipment that is out of service, with a "rebuild without them" action that asks for confirmation first (FR-22, FR-51). Staff see a "Must review" badge and filter in the plan reviews queue, flagged plans sorted first, a banner on the plan review page, and the plan at the top of the staff Overview (FR-56). The flag never blocks publication or access (FR-18) and the system never declares a plan good or bad: whether a plan lacks something useful for the member is the trainer's own professional judgement, supported only by the muscle map (FR-54).
- FR-54: The plan review page shows a muscle balance preview. By default it follows the exercises as the trainer edits them, before saving, counting only what can be done right now; it can be switched to the member's completed work in the 14 days before the plan. It outlines the member's reported injuries (FR-69). It only shows facts and leaves every judgement to the trainer.
- FR-55: The member's Home is a Now screen, not a copy of the plan page: the date and the day's tally, the check-in state (the latest check-in today) and the gym status (open or closed, and the occupancy estimate of FR-37), the next exercise with a Done action, the remaining exercises, the trainer note notice when a trainer left a note or edited today's plan (the notes in full, with author and time, FR-19), the must-review notice (FR-53) and a Monday to Sunday week strip of the days with a check-in (the same rule as FR-36). There is no muscle map on Home.
- FR-56: The staff Overview is an exceptions-first page, readable by anyone who can read every member's plans. It opens with equal summary lines, each a number, a title, one sentence and a link to the page that owns the detail, and it never lists plans or activity itself: "Must review" (FR-53: how many plans, with a sentence naming the next one, its member, date and blocked exercises with their equipment, linking to the plan reviews queue filtered to must-review plans); "Trainer edits and notes" (how many plans a trainer edited or noted in the last 7 days, a window the line states on screen, with a sentence naming the latest author, member, plan date and time, linking to the queue filtered to the Trainer tab, which is the coordination signal in a shared pool with no assignment, FR-19); and, for an admin, "Turnstile" (the failed check-ins today out of the total, or "not configured", linking to the check-in log or to the turnstile settings, FR-61). Below them, a "Today's demand" card tells trainers what today's plans ask of the gym so they are not surprised by missing or over-demanded equipment. Everything on it is counted in plans (distinct plans, never exercise rows or sets) and each count says what it counts. Its muscle half is a body map with a ranked list and numbers (the plans that train each muscle through an exercise that can be done now), heat scaled to the busiest muscle, with an "All plans today" / "Already checked in" filter. Its equipment half is a table, out-of-service pieces first and then busiest first, with two numeric columns, "Plans" (today's plans that need the piece, with a quiet bar scaled to the busiest piece) and "In the gym" (how many of those plans belong to members who have already checked in today), and an "Out of service" badge on a down piece; a plan counts toward a down piece only through an exercise that has no working alternative. The card refreshes every 60 seconds and links out-of-service pieces to the catalog Equipment tab. When nothing needs attention, the lines say so. The plan reviews queue they open offers the filters All, Must review, AI and Trainer: the Trainer tab holds every plan a trainer touched, by an edit or by a note (FR-19), AI holds the plans with neither, and a row shows "Trainer edited" for an edit or "Trainer note" for a note without an edit, all derived from the existing review entries.

### 1.10 Remembered Facts, Plan Safety and Staff Context
- FR-58: The member sees every fact the coach remembered (`f_profile_events`) on the Health profile page, grouped by type with its date and the member's own words, and can mark a fact "no longer true" and later "applies again". Plan and chat prompts skip facts marked no longer true and facts the member has not confirmed yet (FR-27, FR-72); injuries and medication changes are always given to the AI in detail, however old, and are never folded into an "older events" count. The same page shows the member's current profile (the latest onboarding submission), and the update form is prefilled with it.- FR-59: Plan safety. (a) An AI failure never publishes a plan: when the live AI returns nothing usable, generation fails with "AI is temporarily unavailable" and no plan is saved; the deterministic placeholder generator is used only when the generator mode is `placeholder` (the mock/demo mode). (b) Exercises the member ticked off are never silently lost: regenerating or trainer-editing a plan keeps `completed` for the exercises that remain (matched by exercise), and regenerating a plan that has ticked exercises or a trainer's edit first returns a confirmation request with its reason (`trainer_edited` or `has_completed`) that the member must confirm (FR-22); automatic regeneration after onboarding skips such a plan instead of overwriting it. (c) A past plan is history: a trainer cannot edit a plan dated before today ("Past plans cannot be edited", a note can still be added), and a past plan is not re-filtered by equipment that went down later (only plans dated today and later are, FR-53). (d) The member can see upcoming plans (FR-20).
- FR-60: Staff context. The plan review page has a "What the AI knew" card (member name and age; the latest onboarding with physical information, goals, medications, conditions and exams; the unresolved remembered facts with injuries and medication changes first, the member's own words and their dates) so a trainer can judge safety, and shows for each exercise whether the member did it. It has one note field with two actions: "Save changes" (attaches the note to the edit, enabled only when the plan changed) and "Add note only". The trainer can go to the previous or next plan through the queue in the same order and filter, and add an exercise through a searchable picker. The write controls follow the abilities: a plan edit needs both the update-plan and the plan-review permissions, so an admin only reads ("You can read this plan. Trainers edit it and leave notes.") and past plans are read-only (FR-59). The plan reviews queue has a "When" scope filter: Today and later (the default), Past and All dates (the Trainer tab defaults to all dates). A staff Member page (`/members/[id]`, for anyone with the read-members permission, which trainers hold, linked from the Members table) shows the member's profile, membership (FR-40), current health profile, remembered facts (those no longer true muted), plans (date, status, done n of m, notes count and a link to the review) and the last 30 days of check-ins.

### 1.11 Admin Operations
- FR-61: An Admin has a check-in log (`/checkins`): each check-in's member and time, the turnstile result ("Opened", or "Did not open" in a dashed form because it is a technical failure, FR-33) and, for one that did not open, its reason (not configured, timed out, error answer, could not reach, unknown), with a filter All / Did not open. A turnstile health summary of today's failed check-ins out of the total feeds the Overview line (FR-56).
- FR-62: An Admin can edit the gym's weekly opening hours (`/settings/hours`): for each day, open and close times or closed, where close must be after open. The hours drive the open/closed state and the next open or close time on the gym info page (FR-38) and the Now screen (FR-55).

### 1.12 The Coach as the Member's Trainer
- FR-63: The coach answers with reply text and, when it helps, typed blocks chosen from a fixed set: a plan proposal, an exercise picker, an exercise explainer, a safety warning, facts to remember and suggested replies. The coach only chooses a block type and supplies its data; the app draws every block, so nothing the model writes is ever rendered as markup. The server validates each block before it is sent: exercise ids must exist, an exercise that cannot be done right now (FR-17) is dropped, and the difference between a proposal and the saved plan is computed by the server, never taken from the model.
- FR-64: The member can point at things in the app and the coach receives exactly what was pointed at, through one reference system and no per-element coach buttons. What can be pointed at is an exercise (on today's plan or a later day, or a row of a proposal), a muscle, a muscle group, and the whole muscle distribution of the plan. The coach reads all of it against the day the member has on the plan page: that day's plan is the one it discusses (unless a draft is open, which wins), the `@` list names that day's exercises, and an exercise added from a picker lands in that day's plan. Pointing stays on while the member moves between today and days ahead, and a past day is history with nothing to point at. There are two ways in, both ending in chips above the message box: typing @ (or pressing the @ button) opens a list of everything referenceable, narrowed by what follows the @; and "Pick from the page", its first row, closes the chat and lights up every pointable thing on the screen with a dashed frame, so a tap adds or removes it, until the member goes back to the chat. Each chip is sent as the id of the exercise, muscle or group (or as the distribution), so "4 reps is too much, reduce to 3" cannot be taken for a similar exercise. Pointing never sends anything: suggested questions for what was pointed at only fill the message box, and the member sends it. Pointing at a muscle and asking for exercises makes the server prefilter the catalog to available exercises that train it and that no reported injury rules out, and the coach picks 3 to 5 of them with a one-line reason each (the exercise picker). "How do I do that exercise?" and "what are the benefits?" are answered with an explainer written for this member.
- FR-65: A plan change proposed by the coach is a draft that lives only in the member's browser. The member can edit its sets, reps and weight by hand, take an exercise out and put it back, add an exercise from a picker, point at a row and ask the coach to revise it (the draft is sent with every message and a revision comes back whole), and nothing reaches the plan until the member chooses Apply, which sends the final draft to the server (FR-21). The coach never applies anything itself.
- FR-66: Every proposal carries a short memory note written by the coach: what the member asked for and any detail that matters (for example "asked for a plan for 10 Oct, a test to see whether they can do a hard chest and shoulders workout"). The coach rewrites it with every revision, so it includes the important things said earlier in the conversation. The plan is remembered only when the member applies it, as one confirmed plan request in the member's history (FR-27), with that note (the request itself when there is none); a proposal that is never applied leaves no trace, and the request for it is not reported as a fact while it is being proposed. Pointing at a muscle group or the whole distribution lets the member ask whether it is balanced or ask for it to be rebalanced, answered with a proposal.
- FR-67: A proposal, picker option or alternative that holds an exercise conflicting with a reported injury (FR-28) carries a safety warning with its reason, and the coach may add warnings of its own judgement. The member must accept each warning (an explicit "I understand and want this exercise anyway") before the proposal can be applied, may ask for a safer swap instead, and the server refuses to apply a conflicting exercise whose warning was not accepted. Every accepted warning is recorded with the change (FR-71). A warning informs and never forbids: the member can still choose to go ahead.
- FR-68: The member can edit the sets, reps and weight of an exercise directly on a plan that can still be done (today or later, not once ticked, not an exercise that cannot be done), for example when it feels too hard or too easy. The numbers are edited in place, in the same layout they are read in, so nothing moves while editing. A weight is always a number of kilograms: only a number can be entered and the unit "kg" is always shown beside the field, never typed. The change is saved when the field loses focus or Enter is pressed, recorded as a confirmed profile event in plain words ("Member changed Back Squat from 4x10 to 4x10 at 22.5 kg on their own, making it heavier") that the AI reads in later plans and chats (FR-15, FR-25), and logged for trainers (FR-71). Nothing is saved when the numbers did not change. The coach also recommends a weight in kilograms for every plan exercise and picker option that uses added weight, from the weights the member recently used, their hand edits, body weight, goals and injuries, and starts conservatively for an exercise they have not done.
- FR-69: An injury the member reported, once confirmed and not resolved, is shown on the plan page's muscle map for the muscles it names: a Pace Tape outline with a tape hatch, a chip in the hover tooltip, a mention in the hidden ranked list and a legend entry, and the selected muscle's detail quotes what the member reported. An injury the coach could not tag with a muscle is remembered and given to the AI like any other fact, but cannot be drawn or checked deterministically.
- FR-70: What takes time arrives in parts and says so. The chat reply streams as text, then each block as it is complete and validated; a plan being built or rebuilt shows its exercises as the AI writes them; saving the Health profile confirms the save at once and then shows the plan being built. Every button whose action takes time changes its label to what is happening and moves its mark (the Coach Mark, see [DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md)). Nothing is saved from a stream that breaks: a failed plan build saves no plan (FR-59), a reply that breaks off leaves the plan untouched and offers a retry.
- FR-71: Trainers see what members changed without them. The plan review page has a change log of coach changes and member edits, oldest first: the member's request, what was added, changed and removed, and any safety warning the member accepted. A plan dated today or later where the member accepted a safety warning is a **must-review plan** (FR-53) until a trainer leaves a note or an edit on it after that change; the flag is derived on every read, shows in the queue as "Safety warning accepted", in the Overview's must-review lines and as a banner on the plan review page, and never blocks publication (FR-18).
- FR-72: Facts waiting for the member's confirmation (only injuries and medication changes, FR-27) are listed first on the Health profile page, each with the member's own words and Confirm, Edit and Dismiss actions, so nothing is lost when the chat is closed. They shape no plan and are not shown to staff until confirmed.

## 2. Non-Functional Requirements

Kept intentionally minimal, given this is an academic, non-deployed project:

- NFR-1: **Responsive** - usable on mobile and desktop viewports (the member-facing app in particular must work well on phones).
- NFR-2: **Reasonable performance** - no specific benchmark targets; standard SPA/SSR responsiveness is sufficient.
- NFR-3: **Basic security hygiene** - passwords hashed, authenticated routes actually protected (JWT verified server-side), the auth cookie is `httpOnly` and `sameSite=lax` always, and `Secure` whenever `NODE_ENV=production` (off for local Docker Compose demo runs, since `Secure` cookies are dropped over plain HTTP), file uploads validated by type/size, secrets kept out of source control (`.env`, not committed).
- NFR-4: **No accessibility (WCAG), offline, i18n, or scale requirements** - explicitly out of scope.
- NFR-5: **No SLA/uptime requirement** - the system only needs to run reliably for local development/demo via Docker Compose.
- NFR-6: **No password-reset/account-recovery flow** - explicitly out of scope; a forgotten password is a non-issue for this academic demo.

## 3. Permitted Scopes (In / Out of Scope)

### In Scope
- In-person registration: staff decide at the gym (off-system), the person then registers and gets an account at once, with a required onboarding after the first login. The restriction that registration is reachable only at the gym exists on paper only.
- Real facial embedding computation (e.g. face-api.js or similar): on the backend for the signup reference photo, in the browser at the kiosk. Only embeddings - never raw photos - are distributed to the kiosk.
- A face-match confidence threshold with a basic tie-break/ambiguity check.
- Admin-configurable integration point for an external (pre-existing, not built here) turnstile REST API, with check-ins always recorded independent of that call's success/failure, and an admin check-in log with turnstile health.
- AI-generated, AI-adjustable training plans backed by a curated, add-only exercise library and an equipment availability model (N:N exercises↔equipment), planned from the member's accumulated profile and from the demand of other members' plans.
- A conflict check/warning before the AI overwrites a trainer-edited plan or a plan with ticked exercises, with ticked exercises preserved across regeneration and edits.
- AI chat that extracts structured facts into member history (an injury or a medication change waits for the member's yes, everything else is remembered at once, and an applied plan is remembered as one short line; no persisted visible chat log), with those remembered facts visible to the member, who can mark one no longer true.
- The coach as the member's trainer: typed chat blocks, one reference system for pointing at exercises, muscles, muscle groups and the whole muscle distribution, a proposal draft edited in the browser and applied only by the member, safety warnings the member must accept, hand edits of sets, reps and weight the AI reads later, injuries drawn on the muscle map, streamed replies and plan builds, and a trainer-facing change log and risk flag. The AI itself still has only read-only lookups and writes nothing.
- Trainer review/override with a retained multi-trainer note history (shared pool, not 1:1 assignment), notes visible to the member and used by the AI, and a staff Member page with the member's context.
- Personal metrics and gym info: occupancy via a rolling window and check-ins per hour (public), and equipment and muscle demand for staff and the AI, computed from seeded data.
- A muscle map: a fixed list of 22 muscles tagged on every exercise, drawn as a front and back heat map for plans, plan history, metrics, staff demand and catalog coverage, with a radar of the six muscle groups beside it on the member's views.
- A derived must-review flag on plans that hold an exercise that cannot be done, shown to the member and to staff, with a muscle balance preview on the plan review page, a member Now screen and a staff exceptions Overview.
- Real transactional email sending for the registration-completed email with the login link (e.g. via Resend).
- Basic mocked membership status per member (active or inactive), with the lockout: an inactive member cannot log in or check in, and an admin reactivates them.
- Admin-editable weekly opening hours.
- Seeded, fixed-credential demo trainer/admin accounts.
- Explicit, recorded LGPD consent for biometric processing before a face embedding is ever computed.

### Out of Scope
- Real payment processing, invoicing, or subscription billing logic.
- Building or modifying the turnstile's REST API itself - it is treated as an existing external system.
- Persisting/displaying a full scrollable AI chat conversation log (only the extracted structured facts are durable).
- Multi-location/multi-tenant support.
- 1:1 member–trainer assignment logic.
- Real biometric data from real people, and any biometric data leaving the mocked/seeded dataset.
- A real checkout/exit-tracking flow - occupancy is a rolling-window estimate, not an exact count.
- Enforcing the registration restriction (an intranet, an IP allowlist or a staff-issued token): the registration page is meant for the gym's own devices, but that rule exists only on paper.
- Any digital aptitude screening or medical certificate handling: the decision to accept a person happens in person at the gym and leaves no record in the system.
- Deleting exercises from the catalog, editing an exercise's muscle map after it is created, telling the left and right side of a muscle apart, a separate per-muscle focus setting (withdrawn, FR-51: a wish about muscles is a remembered plan request), letting the chat change a plan without the member applying it (the coach proposes a change, FR-65, and only the member's Apply saves it), or any staff self-signup UI.
- A stored "reviewed" or "acknowledged" state on a plan, rejecting or rewriting a plan automatically because of the must-review flag, and delivering any notification (email, push) to trainers or members about it.
- Notifications to staff of any kind.
- Password reset/account recovery.
- Accessibility compliance, internationalization, offline support, per-user timezone handling.
- Production-grade infra concerns (autoscaling, CDN, uptime monitoring, real object storage, hardened kiosk network boundary) - local Docker Compose is the target environment.
